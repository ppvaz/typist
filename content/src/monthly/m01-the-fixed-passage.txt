Once a month, the same passage returns. It does not change, and that is the
whole point of it. A new text would test how well you read something unfamiliar;
this one tests how well each hand and each layout has kept what it learned. The
words are ordinary on purpose: a kitchen table, a window, a kettle beginning to
hum, a cat that has decided the warm spot by the radiator belongs to her. There
are commas and full stops, a few capital letters, a question or two, and the
occasional number, such as the 7 steps up to the front door or the 12 jars of
jam lined up on the shelf. Nothing here should surprise you after the first
reading, and so any hesitation you feel is information about your hands rather
than about the text.

Type it the way you would type anything else. Do not slow down to be careful,
and do not rush to beat last month. If a word comes out wrong, fix it the usual
way and keep going. The numbers are only useful if the conditions stay the
same: the same chair, the same keyboard, the same distance from the desk, and
the same rules about looking. When something has changed, write it down beside
the result. A result with an honest note is worth more than a clean result that
hides a difference.

Over a year, these monthly readings form a small table with one row per mode.
Some rows will rise steadily. Others will stall for a while, or dip after a
stretch of practice in another layout, and then recover. A missing row is not a
zero; it is simply a month when that mode was not measured, and it stays
visibly empty. Read the table slowly. It is not a scoreboard and it does not
rank you against anyone. It is a record of how four different skills, living in
the same pair of hands, are getting along with one another.

When you reach the end of the passage, stop and rest your hands for a minute.
Then look at the result, note anything unusual about today, and leave it alone
until next month. The kettle will still be humming, the cat will still be on
the radiator, and the jars will still be on the shelf, waiting for the next
reading.
