The last bus leaves the central station at twenty past midnight. It is never
full, and it is never empty. There are always a few regular passengers who
nod to the driver as they board and take the same seats every night: a cook
still wearing checked trousers, a student with a heavy bag of books, an older
woman who works at the hospital laundry and sleeps with her head against the
window until her stop. Between them are the strangers, people who missed an
earlier connection or stayed too long at a party and now sit very upright,
watching the dark streets for a landmark they recognize.

The driver has worked this route for nine years. She knows where the road is
uneven and slows before the bump near the bakery without thinking about it.
She knows which traffic lights change quickly and which ones hold her for a
full minute on an empty street. She knows that the student will fall asleep
and needs a gentle word at the university gate, and that the cook will want to
get off one stop early to buy cigarettes. None of this is written in a manual.
It is simply what happens when a person repeats the same journey, carefully,
hundreds of times.

On quiet nights she practices a small game. She tries to arrive at each stop
exactly on the minute printed on the timetable, not early and not late. It is
harder than it sounds. A single passenger fumbling for change can cost thirty
seconds; a green wave of lights can gain a minute. She adjusts her speed in
tiny steps, never rushing, and keeps a mental note of where she lost or gained
time. When she gets it right from start to finish, she says nothing, but she
smiles at the empty road for a moment before turning back toward the depot.

Passengers rarely notice any of this. They notice when the bus is late, of
course, and they notice a rough stop that sends a bag sliding across the
floor. When the ride is smooth and the timing is exact, it feels like nothing
at all, and they read their phones or close their eyes. That is fine with her.
She has decided that the best version of her work is the one that nobody has
to think about, and she is patient enough to keep chasing it, one quiet night
at a time.
